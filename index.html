The Rise of Social Machines — Further Reading

Companion page for the Communications of the ACM Viewpoint by Emilio Ferrara, marking ten years since "The Rise of Social Bots" (CACM 59(7), 2016).

The Viewpoint names several detection tools and generation techniques without formal citation, to stay inside CACM's reference limit for the format. This page is the entry point those references would have been: one citation per technique, for readers who want to go further.

Account-level bot detection

Davis, Varol, Ferrara, Flammini & Menczer — BotOrNot: A System to Evaluate Social Bots (2016)
The tool later renamed Botometer. Supervised classification over content, network, temporal, and metadata signals; the first public web service for account-level bot scoring.
doi.org/10.1145/2872518.2889302
Yang, Ferrara & Menczer — Botometer 101: Social Bot Practicum for Computational Social Scientists (2022)
A practitioner's guide to the current version of Botometer: what its scores mean, where they fail, and how to use them responsibly in research.
doi.org/10.1007/s42001-022-00177-5

Coordination and the field's methodological shift

Cresci — A Decade of Social Bot Detection (2020)
A field-wide retrospective covering the same decade as the CACM Viewpoint, from account-level classification to coordination detection, with a taxonomy of what each generation of methods could and could not see.
doi.org/10.1145/3409116

Synthetic faces and images: from GANs to diffusion

Goodfellow et al. — Generative Adversarial Nets (2014)
The foundational paper introducing generative adversarial networks (GANs), the technique behind the earliest wave of synthetic profile photos discussed in the piece.
papers.nips.cc
Karras, Laine & Aila — A Style-Based Generator Architecture for GANs (StyleGAN, 2019)
The architecture behind the high-resolution, photorealistic synthetic faces that made GAN-generated profile photos hard to distinguish from real ones by 2019.
openaccess.thecvf.com
Rombach, Blattmann, Lorenz, Esser & Ommer — High-Resolution Image Synthesis with Latent Diffusion Models (2022)
The diffusion-model architecture (Stable Diffusion) that superseded GANs as the dominant technique behind the synthetic images in Figure 2 and current-generation information operations more broadly.
doi.org/10.1109/CVPR52688.2022.01042

AI-generated text: detection and its limits

Gehrmann, Strobelt & Rush — GLTR: Statistical Detection and Visualization of Generated Text (2019)
An early tool for spotting machine-generated text from its statistical fingerprint, from the pre-LLM era when detection was still tractable at the sentence level.
aclanthology.org/P19-3019
Kirchenbauer, Geiping, Wen, Katz, Miers & Goldstein — A Watermark for Large Language Models (2023)
The provenance-based approach the field shifted to once post-hoc statistical detectors like GLTR stopped working against LLM-fluent text: mark generation at the source instead of trying to catch it after the fact.
proceedings.mlr.press

The 2016 predecessor and its findings

Ferrara, Varol, Davis, Menczer & Flammini — The Rise of Social Bots (2016)
The original CACM article this Viewpoint revisits ten years later.
doi.org/10.1145/2818717
Bessi & Ferrara — Social Bots Distort the 2016 U.S. Presidential Election Online Discussion (2016)
Estimated roughly 400,000 accounts, about 15% of users in the conversation, were likely bots producing about one-fifth of the tweet volume.
doi.org/10.5210/fm.v21i11.7090
Varol, Ferrara, Davis, Flammini & Menczer — Online Human-Bot Interactions (2017)
Estimated that between 9% and 15% of active Twitter accounts were bots overall.
doi.org/10.1609/icwsm.v11i1.14871
Ferrara — The History of Digital Spam (2019)
Traces spam from email to social bots, the throughline the Viewpoint picks up in describing bots as digital spam's direct descendant.
doi.org/10.1145/3299768
Pozzana & Ferrara — Measuring Bot and Human Behavioral Dynamics (2020)
Shows bot behavior measurably evolving toward human mimicry between election cycles, the trend the Viewpoint cites as blurring the bot/cyborg line.
doi.org/10.3389/fphy.2020.00125

Coordinated inauthentic behavior at scale

Shao, Ciampaglia, Varol, Yang, Flammini & Menczer — The Spread of Low-Credibility Content by Social Bots (2018)
Bots disproportionately amplified low-credibility content in the run-up to the 2016 election, the finding the Viewpoint cites as convergent evidence across groups.
doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06930-7
Gorwa & Guilbeault — Unpacking the Social Media Bot: A Typology to Guide Research and Policy (2020)
Names the definitional muddle the Viewpoint flags: "bot" bundling crawlers, chatbots, spammers, sockpuppets, and cyborg hybrids under one word.
doi.org/10.1002/poi3.184
Ng & Carley — Bots, Bias, and Influence: The Hidden Architects of Social Media (2026)
A book-length history of the field the Viewpoint summarizes in a page, covering linguistic, network, and coordination signatures of bot behavior.
Cambridge Scholars Publishing, ISBN 9781036473112
Luceri, Deb, Giordano & Ferrara — Evolution of Bot and Human Behavior During Elections (2019)
Tracks how automated and human account behavior converged across election cycles, early evidence for the mimicry trend later confirmed in Pozzana & Ferrara (2020).
doi.org/10.5210/fm.v24i9.10213
Ng & Carley — A Combined Synchronization Index for Evaluating Collective Action on Social Media (2023)
A quantitative measure of account synchrony, the kind of signal the Viewpoint's "measure coordination honestly" priority calls for, applied to distinguishing organic from inauthentic collective action.
doi.org/10.1007/s41109-022-00526-3

Bots in political and public-health discourse

Broniatowski et al. — Weaponized Health Communication: Twitter Bots and Russian Trolls Amplify the Vaccine Debate (2018)
Found bots and trolls injecting both sides of the vaccine debate, part of the convergent evidence the Viewpoint cites for automated amplification beyond electoral politics.
doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.2018.304567
Ferrara — What Types of COVID-19 Conspiracies Are Populated by Twitter Bots? (2020)
The study the Viewpoint references when it says viral claims about bot prevalence in the COVID-19 conversation outran what the instruments could support.
doi.org/10.5210/fm.v25i6.10633

AI-era bot detection

Ferrara — Social Bot Detection in the Age of ChatGPT: Challenges and Opportunities (2023)
Argues that LLM-generated content defeats per-message AI-text detection, the claim the Viewpoint traces to this 2023 piece and says the threat-intelligence record has since reinforced.
doi.org/10.5210/fm.v28i6.13185

Generative AI, trust, and information operations

Ferrara — The Generative AI Paradox (2026)
Argues that GenAI's deeper harm is the erosion of trust and the corrosion of information verification, the piece behind the Viewpoint's closing argument that corrosion requires no minds to be changed.
doi.org/10.3390/fi18020073
OpenAI — Disrupting Malicious Uses of AI (2026)
A later threat-intelligence update in the same series the Viewpoint cites for 2024, documenting continued state-linked and influence-as-a-service use of AI models.
openai.com

Open datasets for researchers

Davis et al. — OSoMe: The IUNI Observatory on Social Media (2016)
The infrastructure behind Botometer and related tools; the researcher-access model the Viewpoint says the field lost and is fighting to rebuild.
doi.org/10.7717/peerj-cs.87
OSoMe, Indiana University — Introducing Botometer X (2024)
How Botometer adapted after Twitter's API repricing, the access shock the Viewpoint describes as sending a decade of open measurement infrastructure dark.
osome.iu.edu
Chen, Lerman & Ferrara — Tracking Social Media Discourse About the COVID-19 Pandemic (2020)
The lab's COVID-19 Twitter dataset the Viewpoint cites as an example of collection infrastructure built while platforms were open, later reused worldwide.
doi.org/10.2196/19273
Chen, Deb & Ferrara — #Election2020: The First Public Twitter Dataset on the 2020 US Presidential Election (2021)
One of the election-integrity collections the Viewpoint says has been reused by teams that could not have collected the data themselves.
doi.org/10.1007/s42001-021-00117-9
Balasubramanian, Zou, Narayana, You, Luceri & Ferrara — A Public Dataset Tracking Social Media Discourse About the 2024 U.S. Presidential Election on Twitter/X (2025)
The 2024-cycle successor to #Election2020, collected under the post-API-repricing access regime the Viewpoint describes.
arxiv.org/abs/2411.00376
Blas, Luceri & Ferrara — Unearthing a Billion Telegram Posts About the 2024 U.S. Presidential Election (2025)
A billion-post public dataset for exactly the kind of platform the Viewpoint says bot and astroturfing measurement barely reaches: Telegram.
doi.org/10.1145/3701716.3715297

Cited in the Viewpoint

Minici, Cinus, Luceri & Ferrara — Uncovering Coordinated Cross-Platform Information Operations (2024)
Source of Figure 2(a)-(d) in the Viewpoint: the coordinated-account and mock-news-site artifacts from a real 2024 network, detected from co-sharing behavior alone.
doi.org/10.5210/fm.v29i11.13831
dotNex — Influence Operations on TikTok During the Iran War (2026)
Source of Figure 2(e)-(k): the AI-generated soldier personas and their shared production-pipeline signature.
reports.dotnex.ai
